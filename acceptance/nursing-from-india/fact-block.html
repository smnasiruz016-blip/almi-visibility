What the UK regulator actually requires

The Nursing and Midwifery Council sets its OET requirement per sub-test, not as an overall score. You need at least grade B (350 or above) for reading, listening and speaking, and at least grade C+ (300 or above) for writing. Writing is the only sub-test with a lower bar, and in practice it is the one candidates most often miss. Source: Nursing and Midwifery Council, Accepted English language tests — OET, read 10 September 2026. That page records its own last update as 8 March 2024.

You do not have to clear every sub-test in one sitting. The NMC allows scores to be combined across two OET sittings, provided that between the two you achieve one reading score of at least grade B, one listening score of at least grade B, one speaking score of at least grade B, and one writing score of at least grade C+. This matters for planning: a candidate who has already met three sub-tests can re-sit for the fourth alone rather than repeating the whole test. Source: Nursing and Midwifery Council, Accepted English language tests — OET, read 10 September 2026.

An English language test is only one of three routes the NMC accepts. The second is a pre-registration nurse, midwife or nursing associate qualification that was taught and examined in English. The third is one year of recent practice as a nurse, midwife or nursing associate in a country where English is the majority-spoken language. If either of the other two applies to you, the test may not be necessary at all. Source: Nursing and Midwifery Council, English language requirements, read 10 September 2026.

The qualification route is stricter than it first sounds, and the evidence is a transcript rather than a statement. The transcript must show that you spent at least half of your time interacting with patients, service users, their families and other healthcare professionals, and that at least 75 per cent of those interactions were in English. A course prospectus describing English as the medium of instruction is not what is being asked for. Source: Nursing and Midwifery Council, Qualified in English, read 10 September 2026.

What is different because you qualified in India

Registration with the NMC and permission to enter the UK are two separate requirements, and the second one treats nationality differently. Under the Immigration Rules, Appendix English Language, rule EL 4.1, an applicant meets the English language requirement automatically if they are a national of a listed majority-English-speaking country. That list is Antigua and Barbuda, Australia, The Bahamas, Barbados, Belize, The British Overseas Territories, Canada, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Jamaica, Malta, New Zealand, St Kitts and Nevis, St Lucia, St Vincent and the Grenadines, Trinidad and Tobago, and the United States of America. India is not on it. English being an official language of India, and the whole of your nursing degree having been taught in English, does not change this: the exemption is by nationality, not by the language you were educated in. Source: GOV.UK, Immigration Rules Appendix English Language, read 10 September 2026.
